## Step 2: Start Bitcoin Core in *regtest* mode (Mac)

Open **Terminal**.

Run:

```bash
/Applications/Bitcoin-Qt.app/Contents/MacOS/Bitcoin-Qt   -regtest   -fallbackfee=0.0001
```

What this does:

* Starts Bitcoin Core on a **private local blockchain**
* Genesis is Bitcoin’s regtest genesis (not mainnet)
* No peers
* No difficulty
* Blocks only exist on your machine

You’ve now entered Bitcoin’s “sandbox universe”.

## Step 20: Why Bitcoin hates configurability

Try to change:

* block interval
* coinbase maturity
* supply curve

You can’t — without recompiling.

Bitcoin locks these in because:

* configurability = ambiguity
* ambiguity = attack surface
* attack surface = governance capture

This explains why:

* Bitcoin resists “features”
* Bitcoin prefers forks over flags
* Bitcoin breaks backward compatibility *only by pain*

Checkpoint blocks in RCP are a conscious rejection of this philosophy — but now you can see the tradeoff clearly.
